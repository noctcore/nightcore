## Two different verifications

The word "verify" covers two mechanisms in Nightcore, and confusing them makes
the product hard to reason about.

### 1. Per-task verification, on the board

Every Build/TDD task passes through a `Verifying` state before it can be `Done`.
This is where the deterministic gate battery and the independent reviewer agent
run, and it produces the **Trust Report** — the receipt for that one task.

This happens automatically, on every task, whether or not you ever open a pull
request.

→ [How a change earns its merge](../../governance/gates/) for the full ordered
battery.

### 2. PR review, on the pull request

The **PR Review** destination is a separate scan family that reads *the diff of
a pull request* through five lenses (`security`, `logic`, `structure`, `tests`,
`contracts`), corroborates findings across them, and synthesises a merge
verdict.

Crucially, its verdict is **clamped**: the model proposes a verdict, and a
mechanical rubric bounds that proposal to a band derived from the worst finding
severity present. A `critical` finding cannot come back as `ready`, no matter
how confidently the model writes.

Nothing is posted to GitHub until you confirm — there is no auto-post path.

→ [PR review and the verdict clamp](../../reference/pr-review/)

## Why both exist

They see different things.

Per-task verification sees **your project's own rules** — the checks you armed
in [Enforce](../enforce/), your diff budget, your strictness ratchet, your
anti-gaming sweep. It runs before anything leaves the worktree, and it is
cheap because most of it is deterministic.

PR review sees **the diff as a reviewer would**, without the task's context
talking it into anything. It is the second opinion, and it is the one that
produces comments other humans read.

A change that passes both has: satisfied the rules your repo declares, survived
an independent reviewer with no stake in the task, and been read once more as a
standalone diff.

## Where the honesty is

- The reviewer agent is a model. It fails safe — an unparseable verdict is
  treated as a failure, never as a pass — but "PASS" is still a judgement.
- The clamp bounds the verdict by the severities the scan *found*. It cannot
  bound it by severities the scan missed.
- Merging still runs the separate pre-merge readiness gauntlet over your
  project's own scripts, and that gauntlet **stops at the first failure** —
  later steps report as skipped, not as passed.
