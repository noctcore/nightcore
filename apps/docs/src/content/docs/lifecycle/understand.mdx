## Two scans, two different questions

This stage runs two of Nightcore's five [scan families](../../reference/scans/),
and they answer different questions.

### Find — where are the problems?

The Insight scan sweeps the repo across **nine categories**:

`architecture` · `bugs` · `refactor` · `performance` · `security` · `tests` ·
`docs` · `ui-ux` · `dependencies`

Each finding is ranked by severity and effort, and — this is the part that
matters — **pinned to a file and a line you can open**. A finding you cannot
navigate to is an opinion; a finding with a location is evidence.

Findings convert into board tasks directly, so "the scan noticed it" and "the
board is working on it" are one action apart.

Insight can also run scoped to a diff rather than the whole repo, which is the
cheaper mode when you only care about what a change touched.

### Grade — how bad is it, overall?

The Scorecard scan grades the same repo **A–F across ten dimensions**:

`architecture` · `tests` · `security` · `error-handling` · `observability` ·
`dependencies` · `performance` · `types` · `a11y` · `docs-ci`

Each grade carries evidence, and each reading converts into a task the same way
a finding does. Where Find gives you a list, Grade gives you a shape — which
dimension is dragging, and whether the thing you were about to spend a week on
is actually the weakest part of the repo.

:::note
Scorecard has **no scope option and no deep mode**: it is a single pass over the
repository. Insight supports both.
:::

## Deep mode

Insight supports **deep scanning**: instead of one pass per category, each
category runs a multi-round loop, is shown the findings already collected, and
is asked for new distinct ones — accumulating and de-duplicating until it
converges (two consecutive rounds adding nothing new, by default) or hits a
round backstop.

Read the backstop correctly: the per-category round cap (default 15) exists
**only to guarantee termination**. It is not a cost cap. Deep mode is
meaningfully more expensive than a classic scan.

## Where the honesty is

- Scans are model output. They are *grounded* — every finding points at real
  code — but grounding is not proof. A finding can still be wrong about the
  consequence.
- Grades are comparative judgements, not measurements. Treat a D in
  `observability` as "look here first", not as a metric to report upward.
- Findings are **read-only**. Nothing in this stage writes to your repository.
  Writing is what [Harden](../harden/) proposes and you approve.
